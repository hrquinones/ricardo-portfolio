# Portfolio Strategy & Content Spec

## 1. Objective

Build an engineering portfolio that positions Ricardo Quiñones as a Senior Backend Engineer / Tech Lead with strong experience in architecture, modernization, scalability, reliability, payments, mobile integration, and measurable engineering impact.

The portfolio is not a traditional online CV. It is an engineering narrative supported by real professional evidence.

## 2. Target audiences

### Recruiters
Need to understand quickly:
- who Ricardo is;
- seniority;
- years of experience;
- core specialization;
- measurable impact;
- how to contact him;
- how to download the CV.

### Engineering Managers / Tech Leads
Need evidence of:
- architecture decisions;
- technical leadership;
- modernization;
- scalability;
- reliability;
- delivery impact;
- cross-functional engineering work.

### Engineers
Need enough technical depth to understand:
- architecture;
- constraints;
- trade-offs;
- implementation;
- results;
- lessons learned.

## 3. Positioning

Primary positioning:

> Senior Backend Engineer & Tech Lead

Core narrative:

> Builds scalable systems, modernizes legacy architectures, and turns complex engineering problems into reliable, measurable solutions.

## 4. Content hierarchy

1. Who Ricardo is
2. Engineering impact
3. Selected engineering work
4. Case-study depth
5. Experience
6. Technical expertise
7. Contact / CV

## 5. Evidence principles

Use real information from the CV.

Do not invent:
- metrics;
- technologies;
- project responsibilities;
- company details;
- dates;
- outcomes.

Where a metric exists, make it visually prominent.

## 6. Strong impact evidence

The CV contains, among other achievements:
- more than 19 years of software-development experience;
- card-onboarding error reduction from 15% to below 1%;
- 90% reduction in provider API queries;
- provider cost share reduced from 42.1% to 1.3%;
- response-time improvement of 50% in a judicial virtual-desk project.

These metrics should be connected to their corresponding case studies rather than presented without context.

## 7. Selected case studies

Initial portfolio candidates:
1. Card Onboarding Optimization
2. Card Platform / Microservices Architecture
3. NFC Contactless Payments
4. Judicial Virtual Desk
5. Legacy Modernization

## 8. Case-study narrative

Each case study should answer:
- Context
- Problem
- Constraints
- Role
- Approach
- Architecture
- Key technical decisions
- Result
- Lessons learned

## 9. Technical storytelling

Prefer:
- architecture diagrams;
- before/after diagrams;
- metrics;
- decision explanations;
- concise technical prose.

Avoid:
- walls of text;
- generic claims;
- technology-logo walls;
- unsupported percentages.

## 10. Portfolio voice

Tone:
- senior;
- direct;
- technical;
- confident but not exaggerated;
- outcome-oriented.

Avoid generic phrases such as “passionate developer” unless supported by a concrete story.

## 11. Conversion goals

Primary:
- recruiter understands profile quickly;
- engineering manager explores case studies;
- visitor downloads CV or contacts Ricardo.

Secondary:
- demonstrate technical judgment;
- make the portfolio itself evidence of engineering quality.
